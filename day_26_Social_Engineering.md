# Day 26 — Social Engineering

## 🎯 Goal
Understand social engineering as an attack vector that exploits **human psychology** rather than technical vulnerabilities.

---

## 📖 What is Social Engineering?
The **psychological manipulation** of individuals to perform actions or divulge confidential information, often for malicious purposes. Exploits human behavior and cognitive biases — a major concern in cybersecurity because no firewall can patch human trust.

---

## 🧠 Behavior Patterns Vulnerable to Attacks
Attackers exploit these psychological levers:
- **Authority** — pretending to be a boss/IT admin/police
- **Intimidation** — threats or pressure tactics
- **Consensus / Social proof** — "everyone else already did this"
- **Scarcity** — "limited time offer / limited slots"
- **Urgency** — "act now or your account will be locked"
- **Familiarity or Liking** — building rapport/friendliness first
- **Trust** — impersonating known/trusted entities
- **Greed** — promising rewards, prizes, money

---

## 🧑‍💻 Human-Based Attacks
Attacks that rely on direct human interaction:
| Attack | Description |
|---|---|
| Impersonation | Pretending to be someone else (employee, vendor, IT support) |
| Vishing | Voice phishing over phone calls |
| Eavesdropping | Secretly listening to conversations |
| Shoulder Surfing | Watching someone type a password/PIN |
| Dumpster Diving | Searching through trash for sensitive info |
| Reverse Social Engineering | Attacker creates a problem, then offers to "fix" it and gains trust |
| Piggybacking | Following someone through a secure door with their consent |
| Tailgating | Following through a secure door **without** consent/awareness |
| Diversion Theft | Tricking a courier/delivery into handing over goods elsewhere |
| Honey Trap | Using romantic/sexual attraction to manipulate targets |
| Baiting & Quid Pro Quo | Offering something enticing (free USB, "IT help") in exchange for access |
| Elicitation | Subtly extracting information through natural conversation |

---

## 💻 Computer-Based Attacks
- **Pop-up windows attacks** — fake alerts urging users to click/download
- **Spam Mail** — bulk unsolicited emails, often carrying malicious links/attachments
- **Instant chat messenger** — malicious links sent via Slack/WhatsApp/Telegram etc.
- **Scareware** — fake warnings ("Your PC is infected!") pushing rogue software
- **Phishing** — fraudulent messages impersonating legitimate entities (see Day 27)

---

## 📱 Mobile-Based Attacks
- **Publishing malicious apps** — trojanized apps on app stores
- **Using security apps (disguise)** — malware disguised as antivirus/security tools
- **Repackaging legitimate apps** — injecting malware into cloned popular apps
- **SMSishing (SMS Phishing)** — phishing via text message links

---

## ✅ Key Takeaways
- Social engineering bypasses technical controls entirely by targeting **people**.
- Awareness training + verification procedures (e.g., callback verification, "trust but verify") are the best defenses.
- Attackers often chain multiple techniques — e.g., **pretexting (authority) + urgency + phishing email** — for higher success rates.
- This sets up Day 27's deep dive into phishing specifically.
